I was never really that into Mario games as a kid, although I did own a Gameboy and had one Mario game which was a collection of mini games both me and my mom enjoyed. And even though that was my only Mario experience as a child, I vividly remember the little pixelated ghosts, Princess Peach trying to catch falling eggs and hot dogs with her pan. Those games brought something special to my childhood. Everything was so magical through those eyes. Funnily enough, we don't perceive it that way until we are adults, so in a sense we never experienced the magic. We think of it when we relive the childhood moments in our adult mind, so it never really happens when you think about it like that.

Which does not mean childhood wonder is gone completely. Mario probably gives me some of that pure, simple, almost dumb excitement that you are going to control a virtual guy for the next few hours and jump through witty and silly obstacles, constantly getting surprised by thought-out little features, colorful sceneries, and overall enjoy an insanely polished product. All of those things are kind of expected from every Mario game at this point. You know what you're in for, and that's another beautiful aspect of Nintendo's approach towards these games.

Recently, Nintendo re-released Mario Galaxy and Galaxy 2 for the Switch, and I knew I was going for it at some point. They came out just when I was extremely busy with my job and music. 2026, February, cold shitty boring February - perfect opportunity to delve into a 2-game Mario bonanza. -80 euros - check, slight uneasiness after spending almost 100e on an almost 2-decades-old title - check, excitement rarely felt by games these days - check.
Letsa fucking go. And what is there to say really, it was a classic Mario game experience. At first you're okay with it, then you play for a day or two and start being impressed, then this weird attachment starts where you begin appreciating all the details. How Mario feels to control, how the little dust clouds behave when he's running, how fucking smooth the planet physics are, how magical the menu is. You start having first morning thoughts about Mario, you think about how goofy yet clean the enemies behave. It's visual and technical overkill and I was into it more and more, where halfway through the main story I already knew I'm going to 100% this bitch before I touch another title. And so I did — in a very thorough ~50hr playthrough I completed Super Mario Galaxy 1. At this point I was almost scared of starting the 2nd part. It surely can't reach Galaxy 1 status, surely it will underwhelm me after this masterpiece of a game. It didn't. The game is fucking amazing, level-wise I liked it even more than most of Galaxy 1's level design. They really just one-upped all of the features they developed for the first title. The only part I wasn't amazed about was how you control Yoshi with motion controls, or at least his tongue. The controls are a bit wonky and sometimes slightly tiring to really make it work. I didn't love some of the Yoshi levels, although there were some very fun ones there too. Overall a 10/10 jizzfest of a game providing the same "ahhh" arc that the first title did.

Halfway through Galaxy 2 I realized I'm not ready to go back to Mario-less life quite just yet. So the plan was to go big — let's play Super Mario Bros 3, Super Mario World, then finish it with Wonder, and simultaneously play Mario 64 and after it Mario Odyssey. I played a lot of it on the living room TV and my roommate was so fatigued from my back-to-back-to-back Mario endeavours. I wasn't though. Super Mario 3 was such a blast, it was interesting to experience the difficulty switch. SMB3 was almost hellish compared to newer titles like 3D World or Wonder, especially for the first few hours. Then you get used to the feel of the controls and it gets easier, but make no mistake, the game is still very difficult. While I didn't use saves mid-levels, I did make a suspend every time I started a new level since dying was a constant. If you gave this game to a 1000 ten year olds maybe 10 would be able to finish it. Then again the target audience was probably a bit older in the 90s. However, difficulty has declined and I find that a bit depressing since I can't read it any other way than that we became dumber and lazier and the bar is slowly declining. That's why titles like Mario are still significant and important parts of gaming culture. While there's a lot to hate about Nintendo and their corporate, monopolizing tactics, the Mario series is a testament to quality, purity, and putting joy and passion in front of bandwagoning greedy tactics. The studio that's been pushing mainline Mario games for the last 40 years should be the textbook example of how to run a dev studio — expand on core concepts, stay true to your vision and creativity, while still providing an experience the masses can enjoy. It's a philosophy lost in modern triple-A studios where microtransactions and absurd corporate tactics have bled deeply into the games themselves, overshadowing if not completely replacing any creativity and heart.

Mario is at first an extremely simple game with laughably predictable plots, but when you go a little bit deeper it's an insanely thought-out project, the result of decades of right decisions resulting in an iconic brand backed by strong tradition and a well-deserved spot in pop culture that doesn't seem to be fading, and I'm grateful for it.
