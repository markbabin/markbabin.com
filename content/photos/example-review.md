Martin Kleppmann's *Designing Data-Intensive Applications* is one of those rare technical books that manages to be both deeply informative and genuinely enjoyable to read.

## What it covers

The book walks through the fundamental ideas behind databases, distributed systems, and data processing pipelines. Rather than focusing on specific technologies, it explains the **principles** that underlie all of them — replication, partitioning, consistency models, batch and stream processing.

## Why it's great

Kleppmann has a talent for making complex topics accessible without dumbing them down. Each chapter builds on the last, and by the end you have a mental framework for evaluating any data system you encounter.

## Who should read it

Anyone who works with data at scale — which is most software engineers these days. Even if you don't build distributed systems directly, understanding these concepts makes you a better engineer.

A must-read for the bookshelf.
